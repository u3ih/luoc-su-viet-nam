# Bạch Đằng (938)

_One river, one tide, and one hidden field of stakes brought more than a thousand years of northern rule to an end._

## Bạch Đằng
<!-- id: mo-dau | kicker: A Brief History of Vietnam · 938 | layout: hero | narration: The year 938. On the Bạch Đằng River, a tide and a hidden field of stakes brought more than a thousand years of northern rule to an end. -->

One river, one tide, and one hidden field of stakes brought more than a thousand years of northern rule to an end.

## More than a thousand years
<!-- id: nghin-nam | kicker: Before 938 -->

After the Triệu annexed Âu Lạc, the land of Giao Châu was ruled in turn by one northern dynasty after another. Uprisings followed one another, from the Trưng Sisters and Lady Triệu to Lý Bí, Mai Thúc Loan and Phùng Hưng.

In the early tenth century the Tang dynasty weakened. The Khúc family, and then Dương Đình Nghệ, won back self-rule. But true independence had not yet taken shape.

## Unrest in Giao Châu
<!-- id: nam-937 | kicker: 937 -->

Dương Đình Nghệ was murdered by his officer Kiều Công Tiễn, who seized power. In Ái Châu, his son-in-law Ngô Quyền, a man of Đường Lâm, heard the news and marched north to call him to account.

Cornered, Kiều Công Tiễn sent envoys to the Southern Han and invited their army into the country.

## The Southern Han fleet crosses the sea
<!-- id: nam-han | kicker: Winter 938 -->

The Southern Han ruler, Liu Yan (Lưu Cung in Vietnamese), wanted to use the turmoil to retake Giao Châu. He named his son Liu Hongcao (Lưu Hoằng Tháo) Prince of Jiao and sent him across the sea with a fleet, while he himself camped at Haimen (Hải Môn) in support.

But by the time the fleet arrived, Ngô Quyền had already killed Kiều Công Tiễn. The man waiting for them at the river mouth was now Ngô Quyền.

## Stakes beneath the river
<!-- id: bai-coc | kicker: The plan -->

Ngô Quyền knew the Southern Han ships would enter through the mouth of the Bạch Đằng. He had his men fell large trees, sharpen them, cap them with iron, and drive them into the riverbed in several rows.

At high tide, the whole field of stakes would lie hidden beneath the water.

> [!TIP]
> **The tides at the Bạch Đằng estuary**
>
> These waters have a diurnal tide: the water rises once and falls once a day, and the difference can reach three or four metres. People who worked the river knew the hours of the tide by heart.

## Luring the enemy in
<!-- id: trieu-len | kicker: Rising tide -->

While the tide was rising, Ngô Quyền sent light boats out to offer battle, then feign defeat and flee upriver.

Hongcao eagerly gave chase. The whole fleet of large ships passed over the stakes without knowing it.

## Waiting for the ebb
<!-- id: nuoc-rut | kicker: The moment | interaction: drain@0.85 | narration: Waiting for the ebb. The Southern Han ships now lay right above the stakes. On both banks, Ngô Quyền's men waited in silence. Drag the tide slider to let the water fall. -->

The Southern Han ships now lay right above the stakes. On both banks, Ngô Quyền's men waited in silence. All that remained was to wait for the tide.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Interaction:** High tide ↔ Low tide · Drag right to let the water fall
>
> The water drained quickly. The large, heavy ships caught on the sharpened stakes, split open, and could not turn. Ngô Quyền's light boats came back and struck.

## The Bạch Đằng runs red
<!-- id: ket-cuc | kicker: Outcome -->

Liu Hongcao was killed, and more than half of the Southern Han soldiers drowned. When Liu Yan heard the news, he wept, gathered the remnants of his army and withdrew.

## A kingdom of its own
<!-- id: xung-vuong | kicker: 939 -->

The following spring, Ngô Quyền proclaimed himself king and set his capital at Cổ Loa, the old capital of An Dương Vương. More than a thousand years of northern rule had formally come to an end.

> The court historian Lê Văn Hưu held that Ngô Quyền, with a newly gathered army, defeated the great Southern Han host, founded a kingdom and proclaimed himself king, so that the northerners dared not come again.
>
> — Đại Việt sử ký toàn thư (paraphrased)

## What remains
<!-- id: dau-vet | kicker: Did you know? -->

The exact site of the battle of 938 is still debated. Most of the stake fields excavated in Quảng Ninh and Hải Phòng have been attributed to the Bạch Đằng battle of 1288, when Trần Hưng Đạo used Ngô Quyền's stratagem again, three centuries later.
