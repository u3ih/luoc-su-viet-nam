# Cổ Loa Citadel (3rd century BCE)

_Three earthen ramparts, a magic crossbow, and a lesson in the loss of a kingdom that is still told today._

## Cổ Loa Citadel
<!-- id: mo-dau | kicker: A Brief History of Vietnam · Âu Lạc | layout: hero | narration: Cổ Loa Citadel. Three earthen ramparts, a magic crossbow, and a lesson in the loss of a kingdom that is still told today. -->

Three earthen ramparts, a magic crossbow, and a lesson in the loss of a kingdom that is still told today.

## From Văn Lang to Âu Lạc
<!-- id: au-lac | kicker: The end of the Hùng kings' age -->

At the end of the Hùng kings' age, Thục Phán, a chieftain of the Âu Việt, united the land of Văn Lang, home of the Lạc Việt. He founded the kingdom of Âu Lạc and took the title An Dương Vương.

The Đại Việt sử ký toàn thư records this in 257 BCE. Many researchers today place the birth of Âu Lạc later, at the end of the 3rd century BCE, after the Vietnamese resistance against the Qin (Tần) army.

## Building the citadel
<!-- id: dap-thanh | kicker: Phong Khê | interaction: dap@0.9 | narration: Building the citadel. An Dương Vương set his capital at Phong Khê, today's Cổ Loa, north of Hà Nội, and had a citadel built. An Dương Vương set his capital at Phong Khê, today's Cổ Loa, north of Hà Nội, and had a citadel built.

The old histories record a citadel more than a thousand fathoms across, coiled like a snail shell, and so it was called Loa Thành, meaning "snail-shell citadel".

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Interaction:** Bare ground ↔ Three ramparts · Drag right to build the citadel
>
> According to legend, the walls kept collapsing as soon as they were built, until the god Kim Quy, the Golden Turtle, appeared to help. Only then was the citadel completed.

## Three ramparts
<!-- id: ba-vong-thanh | kicker: Remains -->

The remains show that Cổ Loa had three ramparts built of earth. The outer one is about 8 km long and the middle one about 6.5 km. The inner one is nearly rectangular and about 1.6 km long.

Outside each rampart lay a moat connected to the Hoàng River. The moats served both for defence and as waterways for boats moving between the ramparts.

> [!TIP]
> **The Cầu Vực bronze arrowhead hoard**
>
> In 1959, in the Cầu Vực area inside Cổ Loa citadel, tens of thousands of bronze arrowheads were dug up. The crossbow was a powerful weapon of the ancient Vietnamese.

## The magic crossbow
<!-- id: no-than | kicker: Legend -->

Legend tells that when the citadel was finished, the god Kim Quy left the king one of his claws. The king ordered Cao Lỗ to make the claw into the trigger of a crossbow. With a single shot, the magic crossbow could kill many enemies.

Triệu Đà (Zhao Tuo), king of Nán Yuè (Nam Việt) in the north, attacked several times with his army but could not win.

## Mỵ Châu and Trọng Thủy
<!-- id: my-chau | kicker: A scheme -->

Triệu Đà pretended to seek peace and sent his son Trọng Thủy to ask for the hand of Mỵ Châu, daughter of An Dương Vương.

According to legend, Trọng Thủy lived in the citadel as a son-in-law, found out the secret of the magic crossbow, switched its trigger, and then found an excuse to return home.

## The loss of the kingdom
<!-- id: mat-nuoc | kicker: Around 179 BCE -->

Triệu Đà sent his army to attack. An Dương Vương relied on the magic crossbow. Only when the enemy drew near did he learn that the crossbow had lost its power, and he had to flee south in defeat.

Legend tells that Mỵ Châu scattered goose feathers along the road so her husband could follow. The Golden Turtle appeared and pointed out that the enemy was right behind them. The king killed Mỵ Châu, then followed the turtle into the sea.

> [!TIP]
> **Two different dates**
>
> The Đại Việt sử ký toàn thư records the fall of Âu Lạc in 208 BCE. Drawing on the Records of the Grand Historian (Sử ký) of Sima Qian (Tư Mã Thiên), many historians today place this event around 179 BCE.

## More than a thousand years
<!-- id: bac-thuoc | kicker: After Âu Lạc -->

Âu Lạc was annexed into the Triệu family's kingdom of Nán Yuè (Nam Việt). In 111 BCE, the Han dynasty destroyed Nán Yuè and placed the former land of Âu Lạc under direct rule.

The period of northern rule began. It lasted more than a thousand years, until the battle of Bạch Đằng in 938.

## Cổ Loa today
<!-- id: hom-nay | kicker: Did you know? -->

Today, stretches of Cổ Loa's earthen ramparts and moats still remain north of Hà Nội. Upper Temple (Đền Thượng) honours An Dương Vương, Lady Shrine (am Bà Chúa) honours Mỵ Châu, and Jade Well (giếng Ngọc) is tied to the legend of Trọng Thủy.

In 939, after the victory at Bạch Đằng, Ngô Quyền again chose Cổ Loa as his capital.
