# Văn Miếu – Quốc Tử Giám (1070–1076)

_The Temple of Literature (Văn Miếu) was built in 1070, the first Confucian examination was held in 1075, and the National University (Quốc Tử Giám) was founded in 1076, under the Lý dynasty at Thăng Long._

## Văn Miếu – Quốc Tử Giám

## The year 1070
<!-- id: van-mieu | kicker: Thăng Long -->

In 1070, the Temple of Literature (Văn Miếu) was built at Thăng Long to honor Confucius and the sages.

Six years later, in 1076, under Lý Nhân Tông, the National University (Quốc Tử Giám) was founded beside the Temple of Literature. The Quốc Tử Giám is regarded as the first university in Vietnam.

## The 1075 examination
<!-- id: khoa-thi | kicker: The first examination | interaction: mo-khoa@0.9 | narration: The examination of the year one thousand and seventy-five. Drag the slider to bring the candidates together at the capital. -->

In 1075, the Lý court held a Confucian examination of three rounds and selected ten men. The top graduate was Lê Văn Thịnh. The reference article records this as the first examination; it is also called the Minh kinh bác học examination.

## Later examinations
<!-- id: ve-sau | kicker: From 1086 -->

In 1086, the Lý court examined men of letters from across the country to fill posts in the Hanlin Academy (Hàn lâm viện); Mạc Hiển Tích placed first. In 1195, under Lý Cao Tông, the first examination on the Three Teachings (Tam giáo) was held. The reference article records six examinations in the official histories for the Lý period.

Under the Trần, an examination for the "Thái học sinh" degree, with graduates divided into three tiers (tam giáp), was added in 1232. In 1247 the first Đại tỷ examination chose the three top laureates (tam khôi); the first-place laureate (trạng nguyên) was Nguyễn Hiền, aged thirteen.

## Today
<!-- id: ngay-nay | kicker: Later -->

Today the Văn Miếu – Quốc Tử Giám stands in Hà Nội. The two gardens of doctoral stelae (bia tiến sĩ) lie on either side of the Thiên Quang Well, with forty-one stelae in each; these stelae have been erected since 1484, under Lê Thánh Tông.

> [!TIP]
> **The sources differ**
>
> In 1247: one part of the article says Lê Văn Hưu placed third (thám hoa), aged eighteen, and Đặng Ma La placed second (bảng nhãn), aged fourteen; the statistics section says the reverse. The reference article also cannot confirm that the Quốc Tử Giám was opened to officials' sons in 1076.
