# Edict to Move the Capital (1010)

_A fleet of boats left Hoa Lư among its mountains and followed the rivers to the land of Đại La. There a new capital was given the name Thăng Long._

A fleet of boats left Hoa Lư among its mountains and went up the river to the land of Đại La. There a new capital was given the name Thăng Long.

## A new ruler of the Lý
<!-- id: len-ngoi | kicker: Late 1009 -->

Lý Công Uẩn grew up at Cổ Pháp Pagoda, raised by the monk Lý Khánh Văn and then a student of the monk Vạn Hạnh. Later he rose to the post of Commander of the Palace Guard (Điện tiền chỉ huy sứ) under the Early Lê dynasty.

In late 1009, King Lê Long Đĩnh died. The court officials raised Lý Công Uẩn to the throne, beginning the Lý dynasty. History calls him Lý Thái Tổ.

## Cramped Hoa Lư
<!-- id: hoa-lu-chat | kicker: The old capital -->

From 968, the Đinh and Early Lê dynasties had their capital at Hoa Lư, ringed by limestone mountains. The place was rugged and easy to defend, but the land was narrow and hard to grow into a large city.

Lý Thái Tổ looked further ahead: he wanted a place wide enough to serve as the country's lasting center.

## The edict
<!-- id: chieu | kicker: 1010 -->

In 1010, the king issued the edict to move the capital. It cited how the Shang (Thương) and Zhou (Chu) dynasties of old had moved their capitals several times so that their realms would prosper. It then said that the Đinh and Early Lê had stayed at Hoa Lư, so the fortunes of their dynasties had not lasted.

> The citadel of Đại La lies at the center of heaven and earth: the land is broad and level, high and open, the people do not suffer from floods, and all things flourish.
>
> — Edict to Move the Capital (Chiếu dời đô) (paraphrased)

## Thăng Long
<!-- id: thang-long | kicker: The citadel of Đại La -->

According to the old chronicles, when the king's boat reached the foot of the Đại La citadel, a golden dragon appeared above the royal vessel. The king took it as a good omen and renamed the citadel Thăng Long, meaning "the rising dragon".

The Lý had palaces, ramparts and roads built. For many centuries afterwards, Thăng Long was the capital of successive dynasties; today it is Hà Nội.

> [!TIP]
> **The golden dragon is legend**
>
> The old chronicles record the golden dragon as an auspicious sign, but researchers today generally understand it as the way people of the past gave sacred meaning to an event, not as a record of a real creature.
