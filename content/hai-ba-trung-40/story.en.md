# The Trưng Sisters (40)

_In the spring of 40, two women of Mê Linh raised the banner of revolt. At a single call, sixty-five citadels rose with them._

## The Trưng Sisters
<!-- id: mo-dau | kicker: A Brief History of Vietnam · Year 40 | layout: hero | narration: The Trưng Sisters. In the spring of 40, two women of Mê Linh raised the banner of revolt. At a single call, sixty-five citadels rose with them. -->

In the spring of 40, two women of Mê Linh raised the banner of revolt. At a single call, sixty-five citadels rose with them.

## Under Han rule
<!-- id: nha-han | kicker: Early Common Era -->

In 111 BCE, the Han dynasty destroyed the kingdom of Nán Yuè (Nam Việt). The former land of Âu Lạc was divided into the commanderies of Jiaozhi (Giao Chỉ), Jiuzhen (Cửu Chân) and Rinan (Nhật Nam).

The Vietnamese Lạc lords still governed their people in the region, but they had to submit to the orders and extortion of Han officials.

## Daughters of the Mê Linh Lạc lord
<!-- id: me-linh | kicker: Mê Linh -->

Trưng Trắc and Trưng Nhị were daughters of the Lạc lord of Mê Linh district. The Hou Han shu (Hậu Hán thư) describes Trưng Trắc as a woman of great courage.

Trưng Trắc married Thi Sách, son of the Lạc lord of Chu Diên district.

## Raising the banner
<!-- id: khoi-nghia | kicker: Spring of 40 -->

The Governor of Jiaozhi, Su Ding (Tô Định), ruled with greed and cruelty. The Hou Han shu records that Su Ding used the law to bind Trưng Trắc. The Đại Việt sử ký toàn thư records that Su Ding killed Thi Sách.

In the spring of 40, Trưng Trắc and her younger sister Trưng Nhị took up arms. Su Ding had to abandon his citadel and flee home.

## Sixty-five citadels

News of the uprising spread. The commanderies of Jiuzhen (Cửu Chân), Rinan (Nhật Nam) and Hepu (Hợp Phố) rose in response.

## Queen Trưng
<!-- id: trung-vuong | kicker: 40 – 43 -->

Trưng Trắc proclaimed herself ruler and set her capital at Mê Linh. The old histories call her Trưng Vương (Queen Trưng).

> The court historian Lê Văn Hưu observed that Trưng Trắc and Trưng Nhị were women, yet with a single call the commanderies of Jiuzhen, Rinan and Hepu and sixty-five citadels all answered, and they founded a kingdom and proclaimed themselves rulers as easily as turning a hand.
>
> — Đại Việt sử ký toàn thư (paraphrased)

## Ma Yuan arrives
<!-- id: ma-vien | kicker: 42 – 43 -->

The Han dynasty sent Ma Yuan (Mã Viện), General Who Calms the Waves (Phục Ba tướng quân), to suppress the uprising. The Han army advanced to Lãng Bạc. The two sides fought fiercely.

The Han army was strong, and Trưng Vương withdrew to hold Cấm Khê.

> [!TIP]
> **Where was Lãng Bạc?**
>
> The location of Lãng Bạc is still debated: some argue it was the West Lake (Hồ Tây) area of Hà Nội, others the Tiên Du area of Bắc Ninh.

## The year 43
<!-- id: hy-sinh | kicker: Year 43 -->

In 43, the uprising was put down. The Hou Han shu records that Ma Yuan beheaded the two sisters. According to folk legend, the sisters threw themselves into the Hát River to keep their honour intact.

Their independence lasted only three short years, but the names of the Trưng Sisters live on in the hearts of the Vietnamese.

## Temples of the Trưng Sisters
<!-- id: den-tho | kicker: Did you know? -->

The Trưng Sisters are worshipped in many places. The best known are Hát Môn Temple, Hạ Lôi Temple and Đồng Nhân Temple, all in today's Hà Nội.

Across the country, many streets and schools bear the sisters' name.
