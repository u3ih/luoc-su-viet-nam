# Children of the Dragon and the Fairy (legend)

_Before there were history books, the Vietnamese told one another of a father of the dragon line, a mother of the fairy line, and a sac of a hundred eggs._

## Children of the Dragon and the Fairy

Before there were history books, the Vietnamese told one another of a father of the dragon line, a mother of the fairy line, and a sac of a hundred eggs.

## A story of origins
<!-- id: coi-nguon | kicker: Legend -->

This is a legend, not a record written down at the time. The story was set down in the book Lĩnh Nam chích quái and in the Outer Annals of the Đại Việt sử ký toàn thư, many centuries after the age it tells of.

Even so, for generation after generation it has been the story the Vietnamese use to answer one question: where do we come from?

## Lạc Long Quân
<!-- id: lac-long-quan | kicker: Of the dragon line -->

According to legend, Kinh Dương Vương was king of the southern land. He married the daughter of Lord Động Đình and had a son, Sùng Lãm, known as Lạc Long Quân.

Lạc Long Quân was of the dragon line and used to living in the water. According to Lĩnh Nam chích quái, he taught the people to farm and to dress, and rid them of the monsters that harmed them.

## Âu Cơ
<!-- id: au-co | kicker: Of the fairy line -->

Âu Cơ was a daughter of Đế Lai, of the fairy line, and lived in the high mountains. Lạc Long Quân met Âu Cơ and took her as his wife.

One came from the sea, the other from the mountains. The story of the Vietnamese people's origins begins with that meeting.

## A sac of a hundred eggs
<!-- id: boc-tram-trung | kicker: Compatriots -->

Âu Cơ gave birth to a sac of a hundred eggs. The hundred eggs hatched into a hundred sons.

> [!TIP]
> **Why do Vietnamese call one another "đồng bào"?**
>
> "Đồng bào" is a Sino-Vietnamese word that literally means "of the same sac". Vietnamese call one another đồng bào as a reminder that all were born from the same sac of a hundred eggs, laid by their mother Âu Cơ.

According to legend, the dragon line and the fairy line could not live together for long. Lạc Long Quân and Âu Cơ divided their hundred children, and promised to help one another whenever there was need.

>
> Fifty children followed their mother up to the mountains, and fifty followed their father down to the sea. According to legend, these hundred children were the ancestors of the Bách Việt (Hundred Việt) peoples.

## The kingdom of Văn Lang
<!-- id: van-lang | kicker: The Hùng kings -->

The eldest son, who went with his mother, was honoured as king and took the title Hùng Vương. He named the country Văn Lang and set his capital at Phong Châu. The country was divided into fifteen districts.

The civil officials were called Lạc hầu and the military officers Lạc tướng. The king's sons were called quan lang and his daughters mị nương. The old histories record that the throne passed through eighteen generations, all of them called Hùng Vương.

## Legend and archaeology
<!-- id: khao-co | kicker: Did you know? -->

The Đại Việt sử ký toàn thư places Kinh Dương Vương in 2879 BCE. Historians today regard that date as a legendary one.

According to current understanding, the state of Văn Lang arose around the 7th century BCE, and is linked to the Đông Sơn culture, the culture of the bronze drums such as the Ngọc Lũ drum.

## Hùng Kings' Commemoration
<!-- id: gio-to | kicker: The 10th day of the 3rd lunar month -->

Every year, on the 10th day of the 3rd lunar month, the Vietnamese remember the Hùng kings. The largest festival takes place at the Hùng Temple on Nghĩa Lĩnh mountain, in Phú Thọ province.

In 2012, UNESCO inscribed the Worship of Hùng Kings in Phú Thọ on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

> Wherever you wander, upstream or down, remember the Ancestors' death anniversary, the tenth day of the third month.
>
> — Folk verse
