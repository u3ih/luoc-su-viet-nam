# Vạn Xuân (544)

_For the first time, a Vietnamese man proclaimed himself emperor and named his country: Vạn Xuân, "Ten Thousand Springs", in the hope that the land would endure for ages._

## Vạn Xuân
<!-- id: mo-dau | kicker: A Brief History of Vietnam · Year 544 | layout: hero | narration: Vạn Xuân. For the first time, a Vietnamese man proclaimed himself emperor and named his country: Vạn Xuân, in the hope that the land would endure for ages. -->

For the first time, a Vietnamese man proclaimed himself emperor and named his country: Vạn Xuân, "Ten Thousand Springs", in the hope that the land would endure for ages.

## Five hundred years after the Trưng Sisters
<!-- id: nha-luong | kicker: 6th century -->

After the Trưng Sisters, the land of Giao Châu continued to be ruled by northern dynasties. In 248, Lady Triệu rose in revolt in Cửu Chân, and she too was crushed.

In the early 6th century, the Liang dynasty (nhà Lương) ruled Giao Châu. The inspector Xiao Zi (Tiêu Tư) was cruel, and the people resented him.

## Lý Bí raises an army
<!-- id: khoi-nghia | kicker: Year 541 -->

In 541, Lý Bí rallied the heroes of the provinces to rise up. Joining him were Tinh Thiều, skilled in letters, Phạm Tu, skilled in arms, and Triệu Túc, a chieftain of Chu Diên.

Xiao Zi was frightened, abandoned the citadel, and fled back to Guangzhou (Quảng Châu). The rebels took control of Giao Châu.

## The kingdom of Vạn Xuân
<!-- id: van-xuan | kicker: Spring of 544 -->

In the spring of 544, Lý Bí took the throne as Nam Việt Đế (Emperor of Southern Việt), set the reign title Thiên Đức, and named the country Vạn Xuân. History calls him Lý Nam Đế.

For the first time, a Vietnamese man proclaimed himself emperor, on a par with the emperor of the north.

> [!TIP]
> **Khai Quốc Pagoda**
>
> According to tradition, Khai Quốc Pagoda was built beside the Red River in the time of Lý Nam Đế. The pagoda was later moved into West Lake (Hồ Tây) and is now Trấn Quốc Pagoda.

## The Liang counterattack
<!-- id: phan-cong | kicker: Year 545 -->

The Liang dynasty sent Chen Baxian (Trần Bá Tiên) and Yang Piao (Dương Phiêu) with an army to attack. Lý Nam Đế resisted but lost many battles and had to withdraw into the mountains.

He handed command to his left general Triệu Quang Phục, son of Triệu Túc.

Triệu Quang Phục led his troops back to Dạ Trạch Marsh. The marsh was wide, thick with reeds, muddy on every side, and in its middle lay a patch of high ground where the army could camp.

By day, the rebels put out every fire and stayed silent, as though no one were there.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Interaction:** Day ↔ Nightfall · Drag right to let night fall
>
> When night fell, dugout canoes slipped quietly through the reeds, raided the Liang camp, and withdrew into the marsh. The Liang troops grew weary and could not fight back. People of the time called Triệu Quang Phục the Lord of Dạ Trạch (Dạ Trạch Vương).

## Retaking Long Biên
<!-- id: long-bien | kicker: Year 550 -->

In 548, Lý Nam Đế died. Triệu Quang Phục took the title Triệu Việt Vương.

In 550, unrest broke out in Liang and Chen Baxian had to return home. Triệu Việt Vương seized the chance, routed the Liang army, killed the general Yang Shan (Dương Sàn), and retook the citadel of Long Biên.

## More than half a century of self-rule
<!-- id: tu-chu | kicker: 544 – 602 -->

The kingdom of Vạn Xuân passed through the reigns of Lý Nam Đế, Triệu Việt Vương and Hậu Lý Nam Đế (Later Lý Nam Đế), and lasted until 602, when the Sui dynasty (nhà Tùy) conquered it.

Though short-lived, the name Vạn Xuân and the claim to the title of emperor show a will to govern itself and a refusal to become a commandery or county of the north.

## The dragon's claw and the helmet
<!-- id: mong-rong | kicker: Did you know? -->

The old histories also record a legend. Triệu Quang Phục held a ceremony of prayer in the middle of the marsh, and the spirit Chử Đồng Tử appeared riding a dragon and gave him a dragon's claw to fasten to his helmet (mũ đâu mâu) when he went into battle.

Dạ Trạch Marsh lay in what is now Hưng Yên province. Temples to Chử Đồng Tử and Tiên Dung still stand there.
